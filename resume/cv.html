Research Interests

Reinforcement learning when feedback is rare or delayed. My research asks what an agent should remember from its own experience, and how it can discover which of its past events actually caused success, without anyone telling it what matters. I want to carry these questions into adaptive decision-making systems and LLM-based agents, with experiments careful enough to tell a genuine gain apart from a benchmark artifact.

Research Experience

Graduate Researcher, Ichise Laboratory, Institute of Science TokyoOct 2025 – Present
Thesis: memory and event discovery for sparse-reward reinforcement learning. Advisor: Prof. Ryutaro Ichise
Studying what an agent should remember when reward is rare and nobody specifies what matters. Built a modular PPO research codebase (Python, PyTorch) in which knowledge-graph, flat episodic and count-based memories plug into one potential-based reward-shaping interface, so they are compared fairly without changing the optimal policy.
Ran ~2,400 seeded runs on MiniGrid: a memory bonus raised DoorKey-8x8 from 3 to 22+ of 24 successful seeds, and a hand-written graph memory beat flat memory on DoorKey-6x6 (48 seeds, p = 0.0026) but not elsewhere. This motivated letting the agent discover structure instead of having it written by hand.
Designed a domain-agnostic event detector, with no knowledge of keys or doors, that confirms each candidate event by counterfactual replay. During ordinary training it found the correct event chain in 48 of 48 runs in each of five games and invented none across 432 control runs.
Designed and ran a pre-registered benchmark of 4,308 runs on two shared lab servers (3,552 scored runs over 11 games and 7 methods with 48 paired seeds, plus tuning; Holm-corrected tests). The discovered chain roughly halved PPO’s training time on UnlockPickup and ObstructedMaze (faster on 47 of 48 seeds), and a perfect chain made it up to 7.5x faster.
Reported the negative half with the same rigor: flat memory was faster in 6 of 8 games. The gap to the perfect-chain ceiling points to a testable cause (the chain arrives late), now being checked with analyses declared before any result.
Showed with a test that the public code of VACERL (ECML PKDD 2024), the closest prior method, never trains its learner on its own discovered bonus.
Retracted my own headline result after a failed reproducibility check exposed an episode-boundary bug worth ~24,000 steps of false advantage; fixed and re-validated it on fresh seeds. In the latest benchmark, two independent scoring programs agreed on every number.
